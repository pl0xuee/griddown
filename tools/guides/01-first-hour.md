title: The first hour
kind: playbook
summary: Before you know how big this is — the actions that are right either way, and the one thing you cannot get back.
---

Something has stopped working and you don't yet know how big it is. The first hour is not for big decisions. It is for the handful of actions that are right whether this lasts three hours or three months, and for not doing anything you can't undo.

## Stop, then look

- **STOP** — Stop, Think, Observe, Plan. Give it ten minutes before you drive anywhere or shout at anyone.
- Clear immediate hazards: fire, a smell of gas, water near live wiring, damaged structure. Deal with those or get out.
- Account for everyone in the house. Write down who is missing and where they should be.
- Treat injuries in order: catastrophic bleeding, then breathing, then everything else.
- Find out whether it is only you. Check the fuse box, then look down the street. A tripped breaker and a regional grid failure need completely different responses.

## Fill everything with water

Mains pressure outlives the pumps by minutes to hours, and upper floors and high ground lose it first. This is the part of the hour you cannot get back.

- Fill the bath, buckets, pots, bottles, jerrycans, the kettle, the washing machine drum. Bath water is for washing and flushing, not drinking.
- A full bath holds roughly 150 litres (40 gallons). Your hot water cylinder holds another 150–190 litres (40–50 gallons) that you can drain off later.
- Cover what you fill. Anything open collects dust, insects and small hands.

## Listen before you act

- Battery or wind-up radio, local AM/FM. Write down the time and what was actually said, not what you think it meant.
- If phones work, text rather than call — texts squeeze through congested networks when voice will not. One message: where you are, that you are safe, when you will next make contact.
- Charge every phone and power bank from whatever power you still have.
- Don't call emergency services unless it is an emergency. Every line you occupy is one somebody else needs.

## Small, reversible, useful

- Keep the fridge and freezer shut from this moment. Every opening costs you hours of cold.
- Unplug sensitive electronics so a surge on restoration doesn't kill them. Leave one lamp switched on so you know the second power returns.
- If it's cold, choose one room now and start keeping the heat in it.
- If it plainly looks longer than a few hours and the roads are safe: fuel the car and draw cash. Both queues are unusable by hour three. If the roads are not safe, skip it.
- Start a written log — time, what you saw, what you did, what you decided. By day three you will not remember day one.

## Don't

- Don't leave on a rumour, don't confront anyone, don't tell anybody what you have.
- Don't run a generator, camping stove, barbecue or charcoal indoors, in a garage, or in a tent. Carbon monoxide has no smell and kills people in their sleep.
- Don't take any decision in the first hour that you couldn't reverse in the second.
