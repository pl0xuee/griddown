A garden started after the lorries stop will not feed you this year. Started now, it feeds you next year and every year after. Both facts matter; plan around the second and be honest about the first.

## Grow calories

- The crops that carry a household are potatoes, maize, dried beans, winter squash and sweet potato. Salad leaves are almost pure water — pleasant, not food.
- Rough numbers: a well-run intensive bed gives about 3 kg of potatoes per square metre, roughly 2,300 kcal. An adult needs about 2,000 kcal a day. Do the arithmetic and you find that hundreds of square metres feed one person for a year, before you allow for pests, drought and a bad autumn.
- So aim to *supplement* heavily in year one and build towards more. A 100 m² plot planted in staples is a serious contribution; it is not independence.
- Plant the fast things first if you're starting late: radish 25 days, salad leaves 30, bush beans 55, courgette 50, then maize and potatoes at 90–120.
- Perennials pay back for a decade and take years to start: fruit trees 3–5 years, asparagus 3, rhubarb and berries 2. Plant them the first spring, whatever else is happening.

## Soil and inputs

- Compost everything: kitchen scraps, weeds, leaves, cardboard, ash in small amounts. A hot heap needs volume — roughly a cubic metre — and turning.
- Urine diluted about 1:10 is a good free nitrogen feed. Use it on soil around fruit, maize and roots; not on leaves you eat raw, and not neat.
- Wood ash supplies potassium and raises pH. A dusting, not a dump — it will make soil too alkaline fast.
- Human waste needs a full year of hot composting before it goes near food crops. Until then, fruit trees and fuel wood only.
- Mulch holds water and suppresses weeds, and water is usually the constraint you didn't plan for.

## Saving seed

- **Only open-pollinated or heirloom varieties come true.** Seed saved from an F1 hybrid gives you a lottery ticket, not the plant you liked. Check your packets now and buy open-pollinated where it matters.
- **Easy savers** — self-pollinating, save from any healthy plant: beans, peas, lettuce, tomatoes. Start here.
- **Harder** — they cross with anything nearby: squash and cucumber (hand-pollinate and tape the flowers shut), brassicas and carrots and beets (biennial, they seed in their second year), maize (needs 100+ plants to avoid inbreeding and roughly 200 m of isolation from another variety).
- Save from your best plants, and always from several — one plant's seed is a genetic dead end.
- **Wet-seeded fruit** (tomato, cucumber): scoop seed and pulp into a jar of water, ferment 2–3 days until it scums over, then rinse until clean and dry on a plate, not paper.
- **Dry to the point of brittleness.** A bean should shatter rather than dent when struck. Damp seed rots or germinates in the packet.
- Store cool, dark and dry in paper or glass. The rule of thumb: temperature in °F plus relative humidity in % should be under 100.
- Realistic viability if stored well: onion and parsnip 1 year; maize, pepper and carrot 2–3; beans and peas 3; tomato, brassica, cucumber and squash 4–5; lettuce 5 or more. Test a batch on damp kitchen paper before you commit a bed to it.
- Label everything at the moment you pick it. You will not remember, and unlabelled seed is a wasted season.
