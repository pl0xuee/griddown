summary: Stop consuming and start producing — food from the ground, fuel worked a year ahead, and skills that outlast stores.

A year in, stores are a memory and the question is whether you produce anything. The households that do well in the second year are the ones that spent the first making things other people need.

## Calories have to come from somewhere

- A garden does not feed you in year one, and anybody who says otherwise has never weighed a harvest. Grow calories, not salad: potatoes, maize, beans, squash, sweet potato.
- Rough scale: a well-run intensive bed yields around 3 kg of potatoes per square metre, roughly 2,300 kcal. Feeding one adult for a year is therefore hundreds of square metres of beds even before losses, storms and pests. Plan for a meaningful supplement, not self-sufficiency.
- Animals: hens are the best return — four to six birds give around twenty eggs a week through spring and summer, and need almost no infrastructure. Expect that to fall away sharply in winter without extra light, and scraps alone will not sustain it — laying hens need real protein, so plan on grain or a way of growing insects. Rabbits breed fast on greens. A goat needs grazing and a year of learning. Anything larger needs winter feed you probably can't grow.
- Fishing, trapping and foraging fill gaps but rarely carry a household. Trade labour for food long before you go hungry.

## Work the whole year at once

- Sow, harvest, preserve, store, and hold seed back — every year, in that order. The failure mode is eating the seed corn in a bad autumn.
- Store for the hungry gap: late winter to first harvest is when the unprepared starve in the second year, not the first.
- Wood cut and split this winter burns next winter. Fuel is always a year ahead; wood at 20% moisture gives you the heat, wet wood gives you creosote and a chimney fire.

## Make the temporary permanent

- Water: a well, a spring, or roof catchment into proper covered storage with a treatment step. Fix it once instead of carrying buckets for a decade.
- Sanitation: a pit that can be moved, or proper composting. Human waste needs a full year at composting temperatures before it goes anywhere near food crops; until you are confident, put it on fuel wood and fruit trees, never on salad.
- Shelter: a stove that burns efficiently, insulation, a dry store, a workshop bench.

## Skills, spares and consumables

- Somebody must learn to sew, sharpen, solder, splice rope, repair a bicycle, splint a limb, deliver a baby, keep bees, save seed, butcher an animal. Teach each other; a skill spread across four people survives one funeral.
- The consumables that quietly end things: needles and thread, files and blades, fixings, lamp wicks, boot repair, glasses (keep a spare pair and your written prescription), sanitary supplies, contraception. Pregnancy without medical care is a serious risk and a decision worth making deliberately.

## Governance and records

- However decisions are being made, make it explicit and reviewable. Written rules, a way to change them, and a common store kept separate from private property with a record. Vagueness is what splits groups.
- Agree in advance how you treat people who arrive. Absolutism in either direction ends badly.
- Keep a real journal: weather, planting dates, what stored well, what failed, births, deaths, trades. That record is the only reason year two is better than year one.
- Keep a box of documents, deeds, ID and qualifications, dry and findable. Normal life may come back, and you will want to prove who you are.
