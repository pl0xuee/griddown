---
title: The first 24 hours
kind: playbook
summary: Establish what happened, settle water and sanitation before they are urgent, and get everyone asleep.
---

By the end of the first day you want three things: a realistic idea of what has happened, water and a toilet arrangement that will hold for weeks, and everybody rested. Nothing else on this list matters as much as those.

## Establish the facts

- Two listening sessions on the radio, not a radio left on all day. Note the source of everything you write down.
- Separate what you have seen from what you have been told. Rumour moves faster than news and is usually worse than reality.
- The questions that matter: how wide is it, is anyone coming, and what is the estimate for restoration. "No estimate" is itself an answer — plan for weeks.

## Water

- Treat everything used for drinking, brushing teeth or washing food unless you are certain of the supply. Boil to a rolling boil for 1 minute (3 minutes above about 2,000 m / 6,500 ft), or use unscented household bleach at 2 drops per litre and wait 30 minutes.
- Plan on 3 litres per person per day for drinking alone, and about 15 litres a person a day for drinking, cooking and basic washing. Count your stored litres and divide.
- Don't ration drinking water. Dehydrated people make bad decisions. Ration washing, and go and find more water.

## Sanitation — decide today

This is the decision that most determines whether your household is ill in three weeks. Make it while it is still theoretical.

- If the sewer still works and the drain runs, you can flush with 6–9 litres of grey water poured in fast. If the sewer is backed up, or you're downstream of a pumping station that has lost power, stop flushing immediately.
- Otherwise: two lined buckets today, one for urine, one for solids, with a cover material (sawdust, dry soil, shredded paper, wood ash) and a lid.
- Handwashing with soap at the point of use, from the first use. See the sanitation guide and take it seriously.

## Food, warmth, people

- Eat the fridge today, the freezer over the next 48 hours, then dry stores. Cook meat that is thawing — cooked food keeps longer than raw, and a big cooked meal is morale.
- Heat or cool one room. Block draughts, get bedding into it, and put a battery carbon monoxide alarm in it if you are burning anything at all.
- Knock on your neighbours' doors, especially anyone old, alone or disabled. Two minutes each. It is the decent thing and it is also the single strongest thing you will do for your own security this week.
- Count medication in days, for every person. Anyone on insulin, oxygen or dialysis needs a plan tonight, while transport still works.

## Communications and sleep

- Agree a household schedule: check-in times, an out-of-area contact everyone calls, a meeting point and a fallback. Write it on paper and give everyone a copy.
- Phones in flight mode, checked on a schedule. A phone at 5% is worth less than a phone at 40% tomorrow.
- Make the first go-or-stay assessment tonight, but don't act on it in the dark unless staying is dangerous.
- Then sleep. Split the night if you must, but nobody should be awake for 24 hours by choice. Exhausted people make the decisions that kill them.
