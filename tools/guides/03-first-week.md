title: The first week
kind: playbook
summary: Stop reacting and start running a household — daily cycles, an honest inventory, and rationing set early.
---

Week one is the switch from emergency to routine. The adrenaline of day one is gone and the work is now repetitive: water in, waste out, food cooked, fuel gathered, people looked after. Build the systems while you are still strong and it is still light.

## Turn each need into a daily cycle

- **Water**: a fixed route and time, containers that one person can actually carry (20 litres weighs 20 kg), a treatment step nobody skips, and separate storage for drinking and for washing. Never carry untreated water in the drinking containers.
- **Sanitation**: sited and built properly this week, not improvised for six months. Pit or trench at least 30 m (100 ft) from any well, spring or stream and downhill of it; covers on everything; handwashing at the point of use; a greywater soakaway away from the kitchen.
- **Food**: one cooking session a day if fuel is short. Cook once, eat twice.
- **Fuel**: gather ahead of use. Wet wood cut today is next month's fuel, not tonight's.

## Inventory honestly

- Weigh and list the food. Actual weights, on paper, on the wall. Guessing at this stage compounds for months.
- Work out days of supply at about 2,000 kcal per person per day. People survive a long time on less, but below roughly 1,500 kcal work capacity falls off sharply and that is the thing you cannot afford.
- Do the same for water treatment, fuel, batteries, medication, soap, sanitation supplies. Write the run-out date next to each.
- Start rationing on day three to five — while it's a plan. Rationing that starts when the shelves are bare is panic, and it always cuts too hard, too late.

## Name who owns what

Give every job a person: water, cooking, fuel, sanitation, watch, children, the sick, the log. Rotate the unpleasant ones. A household where everyone half-does everything does nothing well, and resentment starts inside a fortnight.

## People, not just stores

- Turn the door-knocking into a network: who has a well, a generator, a chainsaw, medical training, a greenhouse, a spare room. Who lives alone. Who is frightened.
- Pool where it obviously pays — one cooking fire for three families burns a third of the fuel and makes a third of the smoke.
- Fix things now: the door, the broken window, the leaking gutter, the shelf. Repairs get harder as you get weaker.

## Watch for illness

Diarrhoea in the household is a five-alarm event, not an inconvenience. Isolate the person, start oral rehydration solution (1 litre of clean water, 6 level teaspoons of sugar, half a level teaspoon of salt), disinfect surfaces with a 1:10 bleach solution, and find the cause: hands, water or food. It is almost always hands.

## Then reassess

- Two fixed news windows a day. Write it down and turn it off.
- Revisit go-or-stay with a week of facts rather than day one's fear.
- If it is anywhere near the growing season, plant something. Radishes are up in 25 days, salad in 30. It costs an hour you still have.
