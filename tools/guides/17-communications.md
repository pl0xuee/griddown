Communications is two problems that pull against each other: getting information, and not broadcasting your own. Solve the first by listening, and the second by transmitting rarely, briefly and without content worth having.

## Receive first

- A battery or wind-up radio with AM, FM and shortwave gives you national and international news while emitting nothing at all. Buy one, and keep spare cells with it.
- In the US, an NOAA weather radio (162.400–162.550 MHz) carries hazard alerts directly from the National Weather Service.
- A scanner or a cheap software-defined radio lets you hear local services and other users, which tells you more about your area than any broadcast will.
- Write down what you hear with the time and the source. Two fixed listening windows a day beats a radio muttering in the corner all day, which only produces anxiety.

## Transmitting is a signal about you

Anything you transmit tells anyone listening that you exist, roughly where you are, and — from what you say — what you have. Direction-finding a repeated transmission is not exotic; it is a hobbyist's afternoon. Assume every transmission is heard by someone you'd rather not meet.

- Keep transmissions short and scheduled. A five-minute window at 08:00 and 18:00 is a plan; a radio left keyed up all day is an advertisement.
- Never state over the air: your address, how much food, fuel, medicine or ammunition you have, who is alone, when the house is empty, or that anyone is ill or injured in a way that makes you look weak.
- Agree simple phrases in advance — "we're fine", "come now", "do not come", "meet at the second place". Not code words that sound like a film; ordinary words with agreed meanings.
- Encryption on the amateur bands is generally illegal, and obvious ciphers attract attention. Brevity and vagueness serve better.

## What actually works, and how far

- **FRS / PMR446 handhelds**: licence-free, low power, and realistically 1–3 km in terrain despite the packaging. Fine around a neighbourhood.
- **GMRS** (US): a licence covers a whole family for ten years, allows more power and repeaters, and is the most practical family radio there.
- **Amateur radio**: needs a licence and an exam, and it is the only route to real capability — VHF/UHF handhelds give 1–5 km on the flat and 10–20 km from a hilltop, repeaters extend that while they have power, and HF reaches across a region or a continent.
- **CB**: licence-free in the US, common in vehicles, a few kilometres.
- **Meshtastic / LoRa**: licence-free, very low power, text only, typically 1–10 km per hop and more with height, self-forming mesh. Excellent for short household messages, and this app talks to it directly.
- Height beats power every time. Ten metres of elevation is worth more than ten watts.

## The household plan

Write it on paper and give everyone a copy. It should survive every phone being dead.

- Two fixed check-in times, and what to do if somebody misses one.
- An out-of-area contact everyone calls or texts — local networks jam while long-distance links stay up.
- A primary meeting point and a fallback, and a time by which to act if nobody has been heard from.
- Where a written note gets left, so anyone arriving at an empty house knows where you went.
- Text rather than call: short messages get through congested networks that won't carry voice.

Runners and written notes cost nothing, emit nothing and don't need batteries. In a small community they are usually the right answer, and always the safe one.
