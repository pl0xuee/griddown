Home is your stores, your shelter, your water and the people who know you. The road is where you have none of that and are a stranger. Most people who leave without a destination arrive somewhere worse. Staying is the default, and the burden of proof is on going.

## Leave only if one of these is true

- The building is unsurvivable: fire, flood, structural damage, no heat in deep cold, or contamination you cannot shut out.
- Water and sanitation cannot be solved where you are, and won't be.
- Somebody needs medical care that exists elsewhere and can still be reached.
- Violence in the immediate area is beyond your ability to avoid.

Being frightened, bored or short of a few days' food is not on that list.

## Going requires three things, not one

- **A destination that expects you.** Not "north", not "my cousin's, probably". A named place, and if possible people there who know you're coming.
- **A route you can actually complete** with the fuel in the tank or the legs you have. Fuel is roughly half of what it says once traffic sets in; on foot, plan 4–5 km/h on road, 3 km/h off it, and add an hour for every 600 m of climb.
- **The means to arrive with something.** Water, food for three days, medication, documents, cash, warm dry layers, a torch.

Missing any one of the three and you are not evacuating, you are becoming a refugee.

## Timing decides more than the decision

- Roads jam within hours of a mass decision. The workable windows are usually the first 6–12 hours, or several days later once the surge has cleared.
- Decide the trigger in advance and write it as an if-then: "if water hasn't returned by day X", "if the fire reaches the ridge", "if indoor temperature is below 10 °C and the fuel is gone". Pre-commitment prevents both dithering and panic.
- Know your choke points — bridges, tunnels, a single pass — and hold two alternative routes on paper. Assume signage, traffic control and fuel stations are absent.

## Sheltering in place has hard limits too

- No water at all is a three-day clock. Solve that or the decision makes itself.
- Sealing a building against an airborne plume works for hours to days, not weeks. It buys you time to move, not a permanent answer.
- If you stay, harden it: water, sanitation, heat, food, neighbours. Then set a review date instead of reopening the question every night.

## The middle options

- Send some, keep some: the vulnerable to a relative or a shelter, one or two capable adults holding the house.
- Official shelters are not a failure. They have water, sanitation, heat and medical staff. They are also crowded and loud, and most won't take pets — bring ID, medication and your own bedding.
- Leaving is reversible if you leave the house sound. Lock up, turn off gas and water at the mains if you're going for good, leave a note where your household agreed, photograph the contents.

## If you go

Travel in daylight, tell someone your route and expected arrival, keep the group together, and stop before you're exhausted. Anyone can walk 20 km once; almost nobody can do it three days running with a pack and no plan for water.
