Finding a stream is the easy part. Running a household on water you carry and treat, every day, without anybody getting ill, is the real problem.

## How much

- **3 litres per person per day** for drinking in a temperate climate. Double it in heat or with hard physical work.
- **15 litres per person per day** covers drinking, cooking and basic hygiene — the humanitarian minimum. Below about 7.5 litres, hygiene starts to fail and people get sick.
- **4 litres (1 gallon) per person per day** is the standard planning figure for stored supplies. Two weeks stored is 56 litres for one person, 224 for a family of four — a stack of containers, not a shelf.
- Don't ration drinking water. Ration washing, laundry and flushing instead, and go and find more. Dark urine means drink more; you cannot train yourself to need less.

## Water already in the house

- Hot water cylinder: 150–190 litres (40–50 gallons) of drinkable water. Kill the power or gas to it first, close the cold inlet, open a hot tap upstairs to let air in, then draw from the drain valve at the bottom. The first litre carries sediment.
- Toilet cistern (the tank, not the bowl) unless it holds a chemical block. The bowl is for flushing only.
- Pipework: open the highest tap in the house to let air in, then drain from the lowest.
- Ice, tinned vegetable liquid, the bath you filled in the first hour.
- A rain butt, a swimming pool or a hot tub is washing and flushing water. Treat pool water as chemically loaded, not merely dirty.

## Making it safe

- **Boiling is the only method that deals with everything.** A rolling boil for 1 minute; 3 minutes above about 2,000 m (6,500 ft), where water boils cooler. It costs fuel, so boil what you'll drink, not what you'll wash in.
- **Unscented household bleach** at 5–6% sodium hypochlorite: 2 drops per litre (8 drops per gallon) for clear water, double for cold or cloudy, wait 30 minutes. It should smell faintly of chlorine afterwards; if not, redose and wait another 15 minutes. Bleach loses strength — it is roughly half-dead after a year on the shelf.
- **Filters** rated 0.2 micron or finer remove bacteria and protozoa but not viruses. Chlorine and iodine do not reliably kill Cryptosporidium. A filter followed by chlorine covers both; boiling covers both on its own.
- **Cloudy water first**: let it settle, then pour through folded cloth or a sand-and-charcoal column. Chemicals and UV are wasted on turbid water.
- **SODIS**: clear PET bottles no larger than 2 litres, laid on their side in full sun for 6 hours (two days if overcast). Only for clear water, and it does not work through glass or in shade.

## What treatment cannot fix

Boiling concentrates chemical contamination rather than removing it. If water may carry fuel, solvent, pesticide, industrial run-off or heavy metals, do not boil it and do not drink it — find another source. Salt water needs distillation; you cannot filter or boil salt out of it.

## Storing and carrying

- Food-grade containers, dated, kept cool and dark. Treated water keeps about six months, and anything you draw again is worth a fresh chlorine dose.
- Never reuse a container that held anything but food or drink. One badly rinsed chemical jug poisons the batch.
- 20 litres weighs 20 kg. Carry two 10-litre containers rather than one big one, and use a cart for anything more.
- Label drinking containers and washing containers, keep them apart, and never put untreated water in a drinking one.
- Roof catchment: discard the first flush after a dry spell, screen the inlet, keep the tank covered and dark, and treat before drinking. Avoid run-off from asbestos, lead flashing or treated shingles.
