---
title: Children, elderly and pets
kind: domain
summary: The people and animals who can't compensate for a bad plan — and who show the effects of one first.
---

Fit adults absorb a lot of disorganisation. Children, the very old and animals do not: they dehydrate faster, get cold quicker, can't fetch their own water and can't tell you clearly what's wrong. They are also the reason most households cope, because looking after somebody is what keeps people functioning.

## Children

- **Routine and honesty.** Keep wake times, meals and bedtime. Tell them what is true at a level they can hold — "the power is off, we have water and food, we're staying here tonight" — and don't let them overhear the adult version.
- **Give them a job.** A child with a task is less frightened. Fetching, sorting, counting stores, minding a younger one, keeping the log.
- Expect regression: bedwetting, clinginess, tantrums, nightmares, a six-year-old behaving like a three-year-old. It is normal, it passes, and punishing it makes it last longer.
- Play and lessons are not luxuries. They are how a child processes what's happening.
- **Fluids**: children dehydrate faster than adults. Watch urine — a nappy wet every 6 hours, an older child passing urine at least every 8. Oral rehydration solution at the first sign of diarrhoea, not on the third day.
- **Infant feeding**: keep breastfeeding if it is happening — protect the mother with food, fluid, rest and privacy rather than suggesting she stop. Powdered formula is not sterile: make it up with water boiled and cooled to about 70 °C, use within two hours, and never make a bottle in advance in warm conditions. Ready-to-feed liquid formula is the safer emergency option if you can get it.
- **Hazards**: candles, fuel cans, medication, generators, open water. Under-fives drown in 5 cm of water and are silent doing it.
- Put a card in each child's pocket with their name, your name and an out-of-area contact. Teach the meeting point until they can say it in their sleep.

## Elderly

- **Medication is the first crisis.** A written list of every drug and dose, and a count of days remaining, on day one. Some drugs are dangerous to stop abruptly — see the medical guide.
- Cold and heat kill the old fastest. Indoor temperatures around 15 °C, sustained, cause hypothermia in frail people over days without anybody noticing it happening. Prioritise them for the warm room and the extra blanket.
- Thirst blunts with age, so **schedule drinks** rather than waiting for them to ask. Dehydration presents as confusion long before it presents as thirst.
- Sudden confusion in an older person is usually infection — very often a urinary infection — or dehydration, or a new drug. It is a symptom to investigate, not "just their age".
- Falls: clear the routes, keep a torch within reach of the bed, fix the loose rug, and think hard before rearranging furniture they navigate by habit.
- Spare glasses, hearing aid batteries, mobility aids, continence supplies, skin cream. Anyone bed-bound needs turning roughly every two hours to prevent pressure sores, which are agonising, get infected and take months to heal.
- Isolation and uselessness do real damage. Give them work they can do sitting down: mending, teaching, minding children, keeping records. Most of them have seen worse than you have.

## Pets

- Water is the same problem as for people: roughly 50 ml per kg of body weight per day for a dog or a cat, more in heat.
- Store as many weeks of pet food as human food, plus a can opener, bowls and any medication. Cats are obligate carnivores and cannot live on grain and scraps.
- A carrier per animal, a lead, a muzzle for a frightened dog, litter and a plan for waste. Most emergency shelters won't take animals — know that before it becomes the reason you won't leave.
- Microchip details current, a tag with an out-of-area number, and a photo of you with the animal in case you're separated.
- Never feed dogs chocolate, onion, garlic, grapes, raisins or anything sweetened with xylitol.
- A dog is early warning, warmth and morale, and it also eats. Decide honestly what you can feed before the food runs low, not after.
